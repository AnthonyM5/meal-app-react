# PawPlate Staple Fresh-Feeding Ingredients (draft for review)

_Draft 2026-07-08. Feeds audit-plan AC #2 (the raw/cooked gap table) and defines the
"whole-food ingredients a dog actually eats" set that USDA import should guarantee
coverage of. **Editorial list — review/trim before it drives imports or claims.**_

Every item below carries at least one credible source (AKC, FDA, PetMD, Hill's, or a
veterinary source). Preparation and canine-specific caveats are noted because they
matter for the app's safety layer and for raw/cooked handling. Items visible in the
reference bowl photo (The Farmer's Dog "Jada's Turkey Recipe") are marked **📷**.

> **How this reads against the toxic list**: nothing here overlaps
> `lib/dog-toxic-foods.ts` (alliums, grapes/raisins, macadamia, chocolate, xylitol,
> alcohol, caffeine, nutmeg, avocado). Those stay excluded.

## 8. Legumes / pulses — include, but flag

| Ingredient | 📷 | Prep | Canine caveat | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chickpeas (garbanzo) | 📷 | cooked | Plain, cooked, unsalted; **see DCM note** | [Sundays: Lentils/Chickpeas/Potatoes myth](https://sundaysfordogs.com/blog/mythbusters-are-lentils-chickpeas-and-potatoes-good-ingredients-in-dog-food) |
| Lentils | 📷 | cooked | Plain, cooked; **see DCM note** | [Pet Food Processing: pulses not linked to DCM (2023 study)](https://www.petfoodprocessing.net/articles/16883-new-study-pulse-ingredients-in-dog-food-not-linked-to-dcm) |

> **⚠️ Legume / DCM context (must surface in the app):** The FDA opened a 2018–2019
> investigation into a possible link between diet-associated dilated cardiomyopathy
> (DCM) and diets high in **peas, lentils, other pulses, and potatoes/sweet potatoes**
> — 93% of reported-case foods contained peas and/or lentils, 42% potatoes/sweet
> potatoes. **No causal link was established** (correlation only), and more recent
> peer-reviewed work found pulse ingredients not linked to DCM. These are legitimate
> fresh-feeding ingredients (they're in the reference bowl), but the app should treat
> heavy legume/potato reliance with an informational note, not a toxicity flag.
> Sources: [FDA Q&A on DCM](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/questions-answers-fda-center-veterinary-medicines-investigation-possible-connection-between-diet-and),
> [FDA investigation page](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/outbreaks-and-advisories/fda-investigation-potential-link-between-certain-diets-and-canine-dilated-cardiomyopathy),
> [AKC: FDA Grain-Free Alert](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/nutrition/fda-grain-free-diet-alert-dcm/).

## How this drives AC #2

`scripts/019_audit_raw_cooked_gaps.ts` (to be written) takes each staple base name,
queries `/foods/search?dataType=Foundation,SR%20Legacy`, classifies every result via
`inferPreparationState()`, and asserts **≥1 raw AND ≥1 cooked** variant exists in FDC.
Fruits and some veg legitimately have no "cooked" FDC entry (or vice-versa) — the
script should treat those as expected, not gaps. Output appends the raw/cooked table
to `audits/usda-food-coverage.md`.
